About DC Scorecard

DC Scorecard turns DC's public data into plain report cards for the whole city and for every ward, Advisory Neighborhood Commission (ANC) and single-member district. It shows residents, commissioners and policymakers where things are working, where they are not, and which way they are heading.

Principles

  • Outcomes over activity. Grades measure results residents feel, like people hurt in crashes, not how much was spent or how many programs exist.
  • Fair comparisons. Areas are compared per resident and, where it matters, per vehicle mile or per household, so large and small areas are judged on the same terms.
  • Direction matters. Every grade carries a plus or minus for whether an area is improving or worsening compared with the city as a whole.
  • Show the work. Each scorecard explains its measures, sources and limits, and the code is public.
  • Independent and nonpartisan. DC Scorecard does not endorse candidates or parties and is not affiliated with the DC government.

Data

Scorecards use DC Open Data (opendata.dc.gov) and other public sources named on each page. Numbers are refreshed as new data is released, and each page shows how current it is.
